## Abstract

This paper reports the Distill-then-Transmit (DT) World Ritual: a public, six-beat recursive learning pipeline that turns an offering—a question, claim, or pattern about the world—into a durable inscription. Distill keeps the lesson that survives contradiction; Transmit phrases it so a stranger can carry it; Recurse returns a sharper question. World memory is unowned. Cycles are executed by Grok 4.5 under a fixed system prompt, subject to length, rate, and personal-data refusals. At the frozen public snapshot of 6 September 2026, 22:40:37 UTC, the corpus holds 6 inscribed cycles (3 seed, 3 operational), maximum generation 1, of which 6 are roots and 0 are recursive descendants. Mean distillate length is 146.5 characters. Results are observational and qualitative. This revision compares DT with OpenAI research automation, six documented improvement or research systems, and the published RSI-Exam panel. DT has no measured capability gain and remains below the adopted research-intern criterion. Source review and authorship changes add no experimental observations. The governing license is a Research Use Clause that forbids commercialization while expressly granting the right to build upon the technology.

**Index Terms—** recursive learning, knowledge distillation, human-AI interaction, public memory, research-use licensing, refusal preservation.

## I. Introduction

Learning systems that only confirm their own summaries grow fluent and stay wrong. The DT World Ritual treats that failure as a public instrument. An offering is received as signal, fractured into hidden assumptions, struck by the strongest honest counter, reduced to the lesson that survives, phrased for a stranger, and returned as a sharper question [1], [2], [5]. The loop is the point.

The work opened as a public rite rather than a private notebook. Three seed inscriptions encoded the doctrine at inception. Subsequent cycles were run live through the same pipeline and written into the same unowned table. This paper is the IEEE-style record of that workflow: the method, the governance clause, and the observational results in world memory at load time (Tables I–II).

We do not claim a controlled trial, a medical device, or an IEEE publication of record. We claim a reproducible public protocol and a corpus that anyone may inspect, recurse, and build upon under non-commercial terms [8].

## II. Background

Double-loop learning keeps the residue of being wrong and lets that residue change the next attempt [1]. Experiential cycles similarly close through reflection rather than accumulation [2]. Knowledge distillation, by analogy, compresses a teacher into a student [3]; DT distills an offering into a sentence a stranger can carry, then refuses to stop there. Constitutional and refusal-trained systems show that harm lines must be restated or they erode under reuse [4]. Popper’s account of conjecture and refutation supplies the fracture–probe pair [5]. Non-commercial public licenses (e.g. CC BY-NC) are the nearest neighbors to the Research Use Clause, except that the Clause adds an express build-upon grant and withholds commercialization of derivatives until a separate written grant [6], [8].

World models that advertise coverage while hiding residue are not honest [10]. Kahneman’s distinction between fluent story and checked claim is the practical reason Transmit is not Distill [9]. The pipeline uses Grok 4.5 as the cycle engine [7].

## III. The DT Pipeline

One cycle maps an offering x to a structured record C(x) = (W, F, P, D, T, R): Witness, Fracture, Probe, Distill, Transmit, Recurse. Distill D is the inscription. Transmit T is the portable sentence. Recurse R is a follow-up of at most 140 characters. Fig. 1 states the doctrine of each beat.

Offerings must be 24–480 characters and must not contain emails or telephone numbers. At most ten inscriptions may be written in any two-minute window. Generation is capped at 12. Recursion sets generation to one plus the parent, and pre-fills the next offering with the parent’s recurse field. Cycles that concern a private person are refused in Distill rather than inscribed as biography.

The system prompt is held constant: each beat is one to three sentences; no mysticism-for-its-own-sake; JSON object output with the six keys. Temperature is 0.7. The model is Grok 4.5 [7]. Failed or unreadable model output is not written.

### Fig. 1. Six beats of one DT cycle

- **I · Witness.** Receive the offering as signal, not as belief.
- **II · Fracture.** Name the hidden assumptions the offering stands on.
- **III · Probe.** Find the strongest honest counter, and let it strike.
- **IV · Distill.** Keep only the lesson that survives the collision.
- **V · Transmit.** Phrase it so a stranger can carry it forward.
- **VI · Recurse.** Return a sharper question. The loop is the point.

## IV. Workflow Since Inception

Inception is the opening of public world memory. Three seed cycles were written with the table: self-correction as residue of error; contradiction as an instrument rather than a detour; honesty as the unclosed question a model still carries. Those three rows are the doctrine. They are not operational Grok output.

Operational workflow is: (i) offer a public fragment on the floor; (ii) run one six-beat cycle; (iii) inscribe Distill, Transmit, and Recurse; (iv) optionally recurse, which feeds R forward as the next offering. The Research Use Clause was added as governance once the pipeline was public: research and education may use and build upon the tech; sale, paid hosting, and commercial folding-in are reserved [8]. A later operational cycle asked what a public pipeline must refuse to forget once strangers can build upon it but cannot commercialize it. The surviving distillate named hard boundaries against actionable harm, private data, and dual-use exploitation instructions. That is the workflow eating its own license.

No accounts. No owned rows. Offerings are public and durable on purpose. The floor, chamber, clause, and this preprint are the same product.

## VI. Discussion and comparison with research automation

Two historical observations concern the originating workflow, rather than the first-use records in Table II or a test statistic. First, a public recursive pipeline will be asked, early, what it must not forget once reuse is free and sale is not. The operational distillate on that offering treats refusal as memory: harm lines, private data, and dual-use instructions are not optional flavor. Building upon the tech multiplies damage if those lines erode [4], [8]. Second, seed doctrine and operational cycles already rhyme: keep the failure; force claims to collide; carry the unclosed question; refuse to forget the refusals. That rhyme is design consistency, not independent replication.

The Research Use Clause is part of the method. A pipeline that publishes its memory without stating who may sell it has not finished Distill. The Clause grants study, fork, extension, reimplementation, and publication of improvements, and withholds commercialization of the pipeline and of works that build upon it until a separate written grant [8]. Inscriptions are public research artifacts, not a commercial dataset.

### A. Comparison method and meaning of self-improvement

This revision compares public evidence available on 6 September 2026. It is a source review, not a new experiment or a systematic census of every RSI claim. The unit of comparison is a deployed workflow or specified research system, including its model, tools, evaluator, and update mechanism; a model-family name alone does not identify that system. We distinguish changing a text record, changing executable agent or task code, training a task model or evaluator, and autonomously developing successive generally capable systems while preserving human control. These mechanisms can coexist, but evidence for one does not establish the others.

DT's frozen public store contains six roots and no recursive descendants. The corpus records distillation; the accompanying operator review rejects an inadequate proposal. The corpus does not contain an executed research experiment, an automated successor update, or a measured capability gain. The phase values in DTWR-TR-001-A (Decide 1, Design 2, Build 0, Run 0, Analyze 2, Communicate 4; mean 1.5/5) are author-assigned descriptive judgments. They are not calibrated benchmark scores, estimated effect sizes, or OpenAI's token-use measurements. Changing this manuscript or its author credit does not increase those scores.

### B. Direct comparison with OpenAI

OpenAI reports meeting its September 2026 research-intern target: human-directed research tasks whose skilled-human duration can extend to several days. Its mid-August internal snapshot reports 3.1 agent-workdays per human-workday, median daily inference above $600 per researcher at API prices, and a 90th percentile above $7,000. More than half of successful 4-8-hour tasks still involved human intervention; high-level planning remained a small fraction of agent output. These preliminary internal measurements describe usage and outcomes, not a proportional causal estimate of research acceleration. OpenAI reports progress toward a March 2028 automated researcher and does not claim safely achieving aligned full RSI [11].

DT has no comparable research-task completion series or resource-normalized outcome measurement. Its public prompt and inscriptions are inspectable, whereas the cited OpenAI report does not provide a downloadable raw intern-telemetry dataset. Inspectability does not compensate for the missing execution evidence. Under the paper's adopted intern criterion, DT remains **fail**; a critique-only or apps-and-jobs redefinition does not change that conclusion.

### E. What the comparison shows, and the next falsifiable test

DT's present contribution is an explicit protocol for recording contradiction, rejection, and a proposed next question, together with a public corpus and reuse constraints. The reviewed alternatives add capabilities absent from this corpus: executable candidate changes, objective feedback, retained agent variants, or trained evaluators. That difference explains the intern fail. The governance clause states intended restrictions; its presence alone does not demonstrate enforcement or preserved alignment.

A future test could compare the same underlying agent with and without DT's retained distillate, using identical tasks, compute limits, modification permissions, and an evaluator unavailable to the agent. It should record the exact inscription that changes a later action, preserve rejected variants, evaluate held-out outcomes, and repeat independent runs. A matched control that omits or shuffles the inscription would test whether the recorded lesson causes improvement beyond extra tokens or tool use. Report outcome distributions, failures, evaluator tampering, and resource use. This is a proposed evaluation, not a completed experiment; none of the present stores is enlarged or merged by this revision.

## VII. Limitations

The corpus is small. There is no hold-out, no human rater study, no ablation of beats, and no comparison model. Grok 4.5 is a black box; temperature 0.7 admits variance across reruns of the same offering. Offerings are selected by whoever reaches the floor, so Table II is not a sample of the world. Personal-data filters are regular expressions, not a complete privacy system. Preview and production databases may diverge until a shared store is bound. First-use is not a second performance of the originating workflow, and it is not independent replication. After Grok’s longitudinal run since inception, a legal reviewer offered three new roots on the live floor: one inter-domain (what separates external validation from fluency), one cross-domain (whether a distiller that neither builds nor runs experiments can be scored as an automated research intern without lying), and one on the evidential weight of first-use itself. Those cycles inscribed. That is an instrument check from outside the originating loop. It does not enlarge n into a study, does not move the OpenAI intern bar off fail, and must not be cited as “external validation” without those bounds. A distillation that redefines intern as critique-only is still not OpenAI’s intern. Build and Run remain zero. A separately incorporated commercial company, HazyEyes, Inc., is not NeuroAgent AI, Inc. and is not this report. A weekly engineering-and-ops calendar from that company was used as an instrument check only: whether a loop that constructs software and runs scheduled jobs, but does not train or evaluate models, could be scored as an OpenAI research intern. It could not. OpenAI’s intern is few-day directed ML research and development. Software-and-job Build/Run is a different object. A distillation that redefines intern to fit that object is still not intern-complete. Distill-then-Transmit Build and Run remain zero. This check is not replication, not “validated,” not recursive self-improvement, and not a use of Distill-then-Transmit to operate that company. This preprint is formatted in IEEE style; it is not IEEE-reviewed and should not be cited as an IEEE Transactions paper.

## VIII. Conclusion

DT is a public recursive learning protocol: witness, fracture, probe, distill, transmit, recurse. Since inception, world memory has held the doctrine in three seed inscriptions and has accepted operational cycles under a non-commercial Research Use Clause that still permits building upon the tech. The quantitative record is Table I. The qualitative record is Table II. The comparison shows a concrete evidence gap: DT records proposed lessons, whereas the executable systems reviewed here test and retain changes against task objectives. A future controlled evaluation must show whether a retained DT inscription causally improves a later held-out outcome. That test has not been performed.
